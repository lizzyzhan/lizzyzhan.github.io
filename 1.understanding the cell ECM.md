# Understanding the Cell ECM

The **Equivalent Circuit Model (ECM)** uses simple electrical components to represent the electrical and dynamic behavior of a battery cell.

---

## Basic Concepts

### OCV: Open-Circuit Voltage

OCV is the equilibrium voltage of a sufficiently relaxed battery cell.

$$
OCV=f(SOC)
$$

The OCV-SOC relationship depends on battery chemistry and is usually obtained experimentally.

---

### SOC: State of Charge

SOC represents how much charge remains in the battery, ranging from **0 to 100%**.

SOC can be updated by Coulomb counting:

$$
\boxed{
SOC_{k+1}
=
SOC_k-\frac{\eta_k I_k\Delta t}{3600Q}
}
$$

where:

- $\eta$: Coulombic efficiency
- $I$: Current (A)
- $\Delta t$: Time step (s)
- $Q$: Battery capacity (Ah)

> **Note:** $Q$ can be treated as constant in a simple ECM simulation, but actual battery capacity changes with aging.

---

### $R_0$: Ohmic Resistance

$R_0$ represents the **immediate voltage response** when current is applied.

$$
V_{R_0}=IR_0
$$

Therefore, immediately after a discharge current is applied:

$$
V_t\approx OCV-IR_0
$$

For example:

$$
4.0V\rightarrow3.9V
$$

I remember it as:

> **$R_0$ → Immediate response**

---

### $R_1C_1 \sim R_nC_n$: RC Branches

RC branches represent the slower **dynamic and polarization response** of the battery.

The time constant is:

$$
\boxed{\tau=RC}
$$

Different RC branches represent different time scales of battery dynamics.

#### My Intuition

I think of the RC branch as a **small side channel with a reservoir** connected to the main flow.

```text
Main Flow
========================>

               ↓
          Small Branch
               ↓
         ┌───────────┐
         │ Reservoir │
         └───────────┘
```

When current suddenly changes, $R_0$ responds immediately, while the RC "reservoir" gradually fills or empties.

After one time constant:

$$
t=\tau=RC
$$

the RC response reaches approximately **63.2% of its final change**:

$$
\boxed{
1-e^{-1}\approx63.2\%
}
$$

So I remember:

> **$R_0$ → Immediate response**  
> **RC → Gradual response**

A **2RC ECM** adds another $R_2C_2$ branch to represent an additional dynamic time scale.

---

### Hysteresis Voltage

Battery voltage also depends on its previous **charge/discharge history**.

$$
V_h=Mh
$$

where:

- $h$: Hysteresis state
- $M$: Hysteresis magnitude

Therefore, two cells at the same SOC may have slightly different voltages depending on their previous charging or discharging history.

---

## Cell ECM Structure

**Sensors measure:**

$$
I,\quad V,\quad T
$$

**Input:**

$$
I
$$

**States:**

$$
x=
\begin{bmatrix}
SOC\\
V_{RC}\\
h
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Parameters for a 1RC ECM:**

$$
Q,\quad R_0,\quad R_1,\quad C_1
$$

**Parameters for a 2RC ECM:**

$$
Q,\quad R_0,\quad R_1,\quad C_1,\quad R_2,\quad C_2
$$

**Output:**

$$
V_t
$$

---

## Simple Example

Suppose:

$$
OCV=4.0V
$$

and a discharge current is suddenly applied.

### 1. Immediate Response

$R_0$ responds almost immediately:

$$
V_t\approx OCV-IR_0
$$

For example:

$$
4.0V\rightarrow3.9V
$$

### 2. RC Dynamic Response

The RC branch responds gradually as polarization builds up.

If:

$$
\tau=R_1C_1=30s
$$

then after 30 seconds, approximately **63.2% of the RC transient change** has occurred.

### 3. Approaching Steady State

As the RC response settles, the terminal voltage approaches its new operating value.

---

## Overall Terminal Voltage

For a **1RC ECM**:

$$
\boxed{
V_t
=
OCV(SOC)
+
Mh
-
V_{RC}
-
IR_0
}
$$

In words:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Terminal Voltage}
=
\text{OCV}
+
\text{Hysteresis}
-
\text{Polarization}
-
\text{Ohmic Drop}
}
$$

For a **2RC ECM**:

$$
\boxed{
V_t
=
OCV(SOC)
+
Mh
-
V_{RC1}
-
V_{RC2}
-
IR_0
}
$$

---

## Key Memory

- **SOC** → Remaining charge
- **OCV** → Equilibrium voltage related to SOC
- **$R_0$** → Immediate voltage response
- **RC** → Slow polarization response
- **Hysteresis** → Charge/discharge history

The Cell ECM can be summarized as:

$$
\boxed{
I
\rightarrow
ECM
\rightarrow
States
\rightarrow
V_{predicted}
}
$$

The predicted terminal voltage can later be compared with the measured voltage to correct the estimated states using a **Kalman Filter / EKF**.
